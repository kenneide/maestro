How it works

From the booth to the stage in under a second.

  1. 1
    AB

    djay Pro shares the set

    djay Pro streams what's playing over OSC: track, key, tempo and position. Maestro listens and follows the deck you mix into.

  2. 2
    Ready

    The DJ cues with one tap

    The Maestro Cue plugin sits in djay's effects. Step the light through Stop, Ready and Play. Audio passes through untouched.

  3. 3

    The musician scans and plays

    Scan the QR code in the plugin and Maestro opens on the musician's phone or iPad. Add it to the home screen and it runs like an app.

Features

Everything a live player needs. Nothing they don't.

The key, in your key

Choose your instrument once. Maestro transposes every track, so an alto player reads C♯ minor while the DJ plays E minor. It updates the moment the DJ mixes into the next track.

Stop · Ready · Play

A three-state cue light from the DJ. Ready shows what's coming next, so nobody is caught off guard.

Live waveform

A scrolling waveform straight from the DJ's audio. See the build coming and feel where the drop lands.

Tempo, bar and beat

Live BPM and a four-beat counter, locked to djay, so you always know where "one" is.

Next-track preview

See what's loaded on the other deck, with its key and tempo, before the mix begins.

Traffic light mode

Turn the whole screen into the cue colour. Readable from across a dark stage, even out of the corner of your eye.

Phone, iPad or laptop

No app store, no account. Maestro runs in the browser and installs to the home screen in two taps.

Local and private

Everything stays on the venue's Wi-Fi. No cloud, no sign-in, nothing recorded.

Built for dark stages

Dark mode by default, a light mode for daylight sets, and high-contrast cues that always say what they mean.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What do I need?

djay Pro on a Mac, and a phone, iPad or laptop with a browser for the musician. Both devices join the same Wi-Fi network.

Does the plugin change the DJ's sound?

No. Maestro Cue passes the audio through unchanged. It only reads the signal to draw the musician's waveform and sends cues on the side.

Which instruments are supported?

Any instrument in C, B♭, E♭ or F: violin, flute, piano, trumpet, clarinet, tenor and soprano sax, alto and baritone sax, French horn and more.

How fast are the cues?

Cues travel over the local network and appear on stage almost instantly, well inside a beat.

Do I need internet at the venue?

No. Maestro runs entirely on the local network. Nothing goes to the cloud.

What if the signal drops?

If the musician's screen stops hearing from the booth, the cue falls back to Stop, so nobody plays on a stale signal.
